The other day I joked to a friend: "If I were a boss, I'd exploit my employees harder than any of my bosses ever did — just look at how hard I work my AI agents." Curious whether that was actually true, I used the `gh` CLI to pull every commit and PR timestamp across all my repositories (40+ repos) from 2025 and 2026, then analyzed the distribution from two angles: **volume** and **timing**.

Then I went one step further: I logged into the **VyBio** workspace on Multica and pulled the agent fleet's run records too. GitHub only shows the output — it can't tell you who was on night shift.

<!-- more -->

The numbers tell a different story than I expected: **I do have a fleet that works 24/7, never takes weekends, and costs zero salary — but the one who never takes a break is me.**

## Data & Methodology

- **Scope**: all 40+ repos across the account, including org repos under `aimingmed/*` and `VyBioTx/*` — not just the blog.
- **Timestamps**: commits use committer-date; PRs use created_at / merged_at. All in UTC+8.
- **Matched window**: Jan 1 – Aug 31 for both years (243 days each) — the only clean comparison. Anything labeled "matched window" uses this.
- **Full-year totals**: 2025: 577 commits / 227 PRs; 2026 (through Aug 31): 1013 commits / 345 PRs.
- **Initial data cutoff**: `2026-08-31T18:03+08:00`. I re-checked on Oct 17; the September–October delta is written as its own section using the repos still visible on the account (see below).

> ⚠️ The re-check surfaced a scope problem: two org repos that contributed heavily to the first draft — `aimingmed/doms` and `aimingmed/Aimingmed_Official_Website` — are no longer visible on the account, and GitHub's search index no longer returns their commits or PRs. So **account-level totals can't be subtracted across the Aug and Oct snapshots.** The first half of this post keeps the Aug-31 snapshot; the Sep–Oct delta is recounted from the currently visible account. The two are never mixed.

## Timing: Definitely "Getting Denser and Longer"

**Days got longer.** Median daily span (first event → last event, matched window): **0.73h → 5.86h**; mean: 4.2h → 9.2h. Days with ≥12h span: 12% → **39%**; ≥18h: 3% → 25%. In 2026-08 alone: **30 out of 31 days active**, median span **15.5h**, longest 23.6h. The year's longest day was `2026-07-04`: 00:20 → 23:59, a full 23.7 hours.

**Days got denser.** Counting each natural hour that had at least one event: the matched window lit 229 slots in 2025 vs **558 slots (2.44×)** in 2026. In 2026-08, the covered slots grew from 10/24 to **19/24** — day to late-night, almost no empty gaps.

**The shape changed — that tells more than any total:**

- **2025**: an afternoon plateau (14–18h) + a spike at 01:00 (112 events). 18:00 (58) was the last high hour; 19:00 dropped off a cliff (13). Work wrapped up in the evening.
- **2026**: 09:00–17:00 filled solid + a thick tail from 22:00–01:00. **00:00 became the #1 hour at 169 events.** 23:00 hit 99, and 20/21/22h each had 58/59/75. **The "quitting time" inflection point is gone from the 2026 curve.**

Matched-window ratios are even sharper: 09–17 events went from 253 → 661 (2.61×), while **19–02 went from 163 → 636 (3.90×)**. Hour 00 jumped from 7th place (30 events in 2025) to #1 (169, **5.6×**); hour 23 from 19 to 99.

The only consolation: 03:00–07:59 is essentially empty both years (1.9% vs 1.8%). 04:00 and 05:00 never registered a single event in 2026. So it's not "24/7" — it's "all waking hours." The baseline still exists, just three hours later than it used to be.

A few details worth calling out separately:

- **Vacation time literally halved.** The longest continuous break shrank from 9.8 days to 7.7 days (2026-03-22 → 03-29). Periods of silence >36h fell from 49 to 36 — rest is not more abundant, just more fragmented.
- **Who the boss serves at night also changed.** Events in personal repos (`SilenWang/*`) were 46% of 01:00 in 2025; in 2026, 00:00 is **86%** personal and 23:00 is **85%** personal, while 16:00 is only 39% personal. The day shift is org work (`aimingmed/*`, `VyBioTx/*`); the night shift is personal projects — a line that literally didn't exist in 2025.

## The Big Correction: The Code Was All Written by AI

I have to correct my own first attempt here. In the initial version, I split human vs machine by checking commit messages for `Co-authored-by: multica-agent`, aider, or opencode footprints, and concluded "agents only carried 1/3 of the increment." That assumption was wrong — **the user confirmed that the vast majority of code this past year-plus was written by agents running opencode locally, which doesn't leave a signature in the commit message.** I had misattributed massive machine output as human work.

Once you internalize that, a more important realization follows: **commit timestamps measure the machine fleet's clock, not the boss's clock.** All those "you personally coding until 00:00" inferences from the first pass have no evidentiary basis.

## The Boss's Clock: `merged_at`

Coding can be outsourced; clicking Merge cannot. So I pivoted to PR `merged_at` as the reliable signal of human presence:

- **<60s = automated**. Of merged PRs, 81% were instant-merged in 2025 vs **34%** in 2026.
- **≥5min = requires a conscious human decision**. These grew from 23 → **173 (7.5×)** in the matched window.

A cron-sanity check on the 2026 late-night approvals (22:00–05:59, n=72): only **19%** fell on multiples of 5 minutes (random baseline 20%), only **8%** landed in the `:00–:04` bucket (baseline 20%), and the timestamps used 38 distinct minutes out of 60. **This is a human hand on the mouse, not a cron job.**

Notice 18:00 dropping from 15 to 4 and 00:00 jumping from 6 to 37. The "quitting time" phenomenon is just as real on the boss's clock as it is on the event curve.

(A necessary caveat: 164 of the 173 ≥5min approvals are concentrated after 2026-05; 2025 had virtually no human approvals between September and December — not because the boss never stayed up, but because the workflow didn't have a "human review" gate back then. The table above uses ALL merges to avoid this confound.)

It didn't fall off. The first 17 days of October had a median daily span of **17.1 hours** — the highest of any month in the dataset — and only 7 of 47 days were completely empty.

(The 2025 column here also covers only the currently visible repos. The two vanished repos' output from this 47-day window is missing, so the multiples are optimistic; the absolute volume and active-day counts are the safer read.)

What says "pipeline" more than volume is the merge shape: 109 merges in this window, **only 4% instant** (21% during May–August), 93 of them ≥5-minute "someone had to be awake" approvals, median wait 72 minutes, and 40% at night.

GitHub commits are just the footprints. To see how the fleet actually works, you have to look at the dispatch board.

I run a separate workspace on Multica called **VyBio** (biomedical research). It hosts **15 agents**, 16 projects, and 1,925 tasks. Here's what the usage dashboard says (UTC+8, as of Oct 17):

| Last 30 days | ~3,000 | 478.6 hours (19.9 days) | 26/30 | ~900 |

What stands out isn't the 3,000 runs in 30 days — it's the **time concentration**. Of the workspace's 673.9 agent-hours for the entire year, **71% happened in the last 30 days and 94% in the last 90.** The "agent era" didn't grow gradually; it landed on top of me in the last three months.

Look at the shape of the load. The 30-day average is **16 agent-hours per calendar day** (about 0.66 of a never-resting employee), but the peaks are a different order of magnitude:

- `2026-10-15`: 112.4 agent-hours, 576 runs
- `2026-10-16`: 1,352 runs, 106.5 agent-hours, **796 failures**
- `2026-10-17` (through 22:36): 80.2 agent-hours, 292 runs

Oct 16 was roughly **4.4 agents working in parallel for a full day** — and 796 failures on that same day. The reasons are very "on-call schedule":

## Conclusion: You're Not Exploiting Employees — You Made Yourself the Bottleneck

Stack the two clocks together and you get something very simple:

**The machine side has no fatigue, only quota; no mood, only a failure rate. The scarce resource isn't compute — it's my attention.**

- The machine side's plate: non-merge content commits grew from 154 → **726 (4.7×)**, attending days from 53 → 144, hourly coverage from 19/24 → **21/24**.
- The boss side: approval wait median **12.2h → 1.5h (8× faster)**, same-day open → merge 48% → **71%**, **45% of approvals happen within 1 hour of the PR**.
- By Sep–Oct it got more extreme: instant merges fell to 4%, median wait 72 minutes, and 93 of 109 merges required a human decision.
- On Multica: ~3,000 runs, ~900 failures, 16 agent-hours per day. **In 2026, 49% of my days required me to show up on GitHub, and 51% of those attendance days included a late-night operation** (2025: 28%, 30%).

It's not that there's too much work to do — it's that there's too much work to **approve**, retry, and re-route when upstream quota runs dry.

```mermaid

    A["Boss: me"]-->|Assign work| B["AI Fleet: 24/7"]
    B-->|"Produce commit / PR"| C["Boss: reviewer"]
    C-->|"Click Merge"| D["Ship"]
    C-.->|"45% within 1 hour"| E["Attention fragmented"]
    B-.->|"~3000 runs / ~900 failures in 30d"| F["Machine clock covers 21h/24h"]
    E -.->|"The boss never rests"| G["I became the on-call schedule"]
    F -.-> G
```

So the quote that started this whole exercise ends up rewritten:

> ~~I'd exploit employees harder than any of my bosses~~  
> **I put my AI fleet on a 24/7 shift, and then scheduled myself as their on-call person so they'd never sit idle.**

The employee isn't tired; the boss doesn't have a day off, and 45% of the shifts require a 1-hour response time. The only real cost in this employment relationship is the boss's attention, reduced to constant standby — the machine has turned "overtime" from a penalty into a default configuration, and the person being configured isn't on the machine side.

## Caveats & Limitations

- **Boss's clock = PR `merged_at` (primary: ALL merges; auxiliary: ≥5min latency subset); Machine clock = non-merge content commits.** All UTC+8. Primary comparisons use the matched Jan–Aug window.
- `merged_at` isn't purely human: GitHub auto-merge and CI-pass → auto-merge also write this field. Two filters (exclude <60s instant merges + minute-randomness test) reduce noise but cannot eliminate it entirely. The "on-call" conclusion should be read as an upper bound; the night/weekend degradation is robust.
- **Commit count ≠ workload.** The numbers include `pixi.lock` updates, blog posts, `via upgit client` batch syncs (21 instances), and 4 FydeOS replays on 2026-01-21 (committer-date vs author-date off by 751h). None represent actual engineering time — which is why this post prioritizes distribution metrics (hour slots, day span, quiet periods, merge timestamps) over raw counts. 1579/1590 commits have matching committer/author dates; replay contamination is minimal.
- **Repo scope changed.** `aimingmed/doms` and `aimingmed/Aimingmed_Official_Website`, present in the Aug-31 draft, are no longer accessible. The Sep–Oct delta and the first-half cumulative totals therefore can't be added or subtracted across; each is valid on its own, but don't combine them.
- **Multica usage scope.** The VyBio workspace's run count / runtime / failures come from the workspace dashboard (`runtime/daily` and `agent-runtime`); tokens come from `usage/daily`. The three views count slightly differently (a task can register in usage but not in runtime), so figures are approximated separately and never cross-summed. "Failures" include retries and are not the same as a task failure rate.
- The `silen_blog` repo alone: 2025 → 141 commits / 46 active days → 2026 → 217 commits / 54 active days / 64 PRs. Growth there is far below the global average, confirming that the volume increase comes from parallel activity across many repos, not just more blog posts.
