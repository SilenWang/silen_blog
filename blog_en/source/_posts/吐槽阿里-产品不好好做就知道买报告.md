A while ago, Alibaba released a coding agent called Qoder, and suddenly a research report claimed it beats Claude Code. I immediately smelled something fishy: this thing barely has any users, so how did it suddenly become "number one"?

<!-- more -->

So I tried to find the original report, only to discover it was a paid research report I couldn't access. A benchmark report that you have to pay for isn't really a benchmark at all — it's clearly an advertisement. Back then I suspected it was paid promotion, but since I had no proof, I didn't think much of it.

Then today I saw another news story: Alibaba's office agent is number one in the world. Same non-public paid report, same synchronized media coverage, they didn't even bother to change the headline. This time I don't have to wonder — it's an advertisement.

If your product were actually good, would you really need to go to all this trouble? A paid report here, some bought coverage there, and with this whole combination, people who don't know any better would think you rule the world. But what's it actually like to use? Big slogans everywhere, ads everywhere, and yet the product is a completely different story.

Anyway, I've figured it out: they don't put any effort into the products, but they're happy to spend money on reports. If that money had gone into polishing their products instead, they might have made a few real "number one" products by now.
